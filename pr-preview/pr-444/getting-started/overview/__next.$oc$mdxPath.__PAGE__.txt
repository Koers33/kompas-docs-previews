# What Kompas Does

This page tours the platform's capabilities for a new user: what you can do today, what is still being built out, and what is planned.

## What is Kompas for?

Kompas runs the day-to-day work of a treasury desk and the teams around it. A dealer captures a deal, a manager confirms it, the platform books the accounting entry, back office prepares settlement, and risk, compliance, and finance see the results in their own apps. Every step is recorded, and sensitive steps require a second person.

## What can I do with deals today?

- Capture deals across the instrument families the platform supports — FX spot, FX forward, money market, term loans, and deposits — with a deal number generated for you.
- Track each deal through its lifecycle status, from capture through confirmation to maturity, with the history of every status change visible on the deal.
- Confirm or cancel a deal. Confirmation is an explicit, permission-controlled step, and the person who captured a deal cannot confirm it themselves.
- Lock contractual terms once agreed, so captured economics cannot be silently edited afterwards.
- Roll over a maturing deal: propose a rollover, have a colleague approve it, amend a proposal, or abandon it. Each rollover records the legs being closed and the legs being opened.
- Record client exit or redemption requests against a deal, and flag conduct issues for review.
- For deals made on behalf of a client, complete the pre-hedging disclosure check that the platform then reports on.

## What can I do with counterparties and clients today?

- Maintain business partners — people and organisations — with their addresses, bank accounts, emails, and phone numbers.
- Manage counterparty records including KYC and AML screening status, and client records with their mandates.
- View and maintain standing settlement instructions per counterparty and currency.
- Handle client FX requests and track them through projects with phases and items.

## What market and position views exist today?

- Browse forex rates, trading symbols, and market calendars maintained in the platform.
- View open risk positions and act on them: propose a lifecycle action on a position, have it authorised or rejected, and trace a position's ancestry chain back to its origin.

## What does the platform record automatically?

- Accounting journals: when a deal is confirmed, the platform resolves the right general ledger accounts and writes a journal entry whose integrity is protected by a hash chain — any tampering with recorded entries is detectable.
- Notes, documents, and attachments on business records, with file uploads scanned for malware.
- Payment requests and cost provisions.
- E-mails and system notifications generated by platform events, with delivery monitoring visible to administrators.

## What can administrators configure today?

- The accounting foundation: global financial nodes, company-level activation with an approve/reject workflow, node-to-general-ledger-account mappings with a readiness check, the chart of accounts, nostro accounts (including suspend and reactivate), and an instrument-effects matrix with a posting preview that shows what *would* be booked before anything is.
- Users and roles, synchronised from the identity service.
- The glossary of treasury terminology, including spreadsheet import.
- The instrument catalogue: instruments are defined globally, then activated per company, then scoped per client — activation is a deliberate, manual step.

## What is only partly available?

- Hedge accounting relationships exist in the data model but are not yet wired into position handling.
- Scheduled background processing is configured but not every job is switched on.
- Change-history tracking is enabled for business partners but not yet for deals.
- Several accounting event types beyond initial recognition (revaluation, settlement, derecognition) are planned but not yet posted.

## What is planned, not yet delivered?

These areas have their data model designed but no working features yet: the settlement engine, cashflow projection and matching, valuation and profit-and-loss, risk limits and utilisation, capital adequacy, collateral, liquidity and ALM, market-data pipelines, scenario simulation, regulatory reporting, and evidence replay. Do not demonstrate or rely on them.
