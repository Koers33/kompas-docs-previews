# Using the Fiori Apps

Every Kompas application on the launchpad is a Fiori Elements app built from the same two screens: a List Report and an Object Page. Once you learn the pattern in one app, you know the navigation in all of them. This page explains the shared behaviour: navigation, drafts, value helps, colour codes, and field states.

## How do I get around? — List Report and Object Page

The **List Report** is your starting screen: a filter bar across the top and a table of records below. You filter, search, and sort to find the record you want.

Opening a record takes you to its **Object Page**:

- The **header** at the top shows the record's identity and status, with the actions available to you (Confirm, Propose rollover, and so on) in the header toolbar.
- Below the header, **sections** group related information — general details, the financial legs of a deal, lifecycle history, notes, attachments. Sections are tabs or stacked blocks depending on the app.
- Child tables inside sections (for example the legs of a deal) can be created and edited in place.

A breadcrumb above the header takes you back. The same layout logic — list, then detail page with header and sections — repeats in every transactional app.

## What are drafts? — Create, Edit, Save, Discard

You never write directly into the live record. Changes happen on a **draft**:

- **Create** starts a new draft. You fill in the form; nothing is saved to the system until you **Activate** the draft.
- **Edit** on an existing record also opens a draft copy of it. Activating applies your changes; discarding throws them away and leaves the original untouched.
- **Discard** deletes the draft. A record you never activated simply disappears.
- **Activate** (sometimes labelled *Create* or *Apply Changes*) is the moment the record becomes real. Platform validations run at this point — if something is wrong, activation fails and tells you what to fix, and your draft stays so you can correct it.

A **page indicator** at the top of the form shows your progress and whether anything is still unsaved. While your draft is open, the record is locked to you: colleagues see it marked as being edited and cannot open their own copy until you finish or discard. Old drafts of your own records are recoverable from the list report's draft view.

## How do I narrow down long lists? — Value helps and filters

Fields backed by lists of options (currency, counterparty, status, instrument) offer a **value help**: click the field and either pick from a short dropdown or open a dialog where you can search and filter the full option list. When the dropdown already shows every option, it is called a *suggested values* list.

The filter bar on a List Report works like a spreadsheet filter: fields marked as selection fields let you constrain the table, and many support ranges ("greater than", "between"). Column headers sort, and a personal view lets you keep your preferred filter and column setup.

## What do the colours next to values mean?

Status fields use a small coloured dot or badge. The meaning is consistent across every Kompas app:

| Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Grey | Neutral or not applicable — nothing to act on |
| Red | Error — action required |
| Orange | Warning — attention needed |
| Green | Positive — all correct |
| Blue | Informational |

So a red dot on a deal's lifecycle status means you must do something, an orange one means you should look, and green means the item is in good standing.

## Why can't I edit this field?

Fields on an Object Page come in three states, and the state is set by the app, not by you:

- **Read-only** fields are shown but never editable — typically values the platform derived (a generated deal number, a calculated remaining term) or fields frozen by policy. A common example: once a deal's contractual terms are locked, the economics fields stop accepting edits even though they remain visible.
- **Mandatory** fields must hold a value before you can activate a draft or run an action; they are marked with an asterisk and an inline message names any that are missing.
- **Hidden** fields simply do not appear for you — often because they only matter in another status, another company context, or another role.
