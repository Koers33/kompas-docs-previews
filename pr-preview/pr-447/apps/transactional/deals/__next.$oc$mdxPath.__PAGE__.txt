# Deals app

Deals is the operational workspace for individual treasury deal records. Capturing a deal is how a treasury trade becomes a governed platform record: you enter the terms of the trade, the platform checks them against your company's setup and business relationships, and the saved deal becomes the record that confirmation, accounting, and settlement build on. Use the app to capture a deal, progress it through its lifecycle, and review everything the platform recorded for that deal—in one place, in the context of the deal itself.

## What is this app for?

One deal is one record: its terms, parties, client attribution, and status, together with the accounting entries, cashflows, rate history, and evidence the platform generates as the deal is processed. The app covers treasury instruments including spot and forward foreign exchange, money market trades, loans, and OTC derivatives. Instead of chasing accounting, cashflow, and evidence records as separate work items, you review them on the deal that produced or references them.

## When should I use this app?

Use Deals when you need to create a deal, review a specific deal, or take the next available lifecycle action on it. Use the relevant master-data apps when you need to maintain shared information—such as a company, counterparty, or instrument—rather than the deal itself. Client-originated requests (for example, an FX or payment request from a client) begin in the [client workflow](/user-guides/client/client-workflows); once treasury executes the trade, the resulting deal is captured and managed here. See the [deal capture guide](/user-guides/treasury/deal-capture) for that end-to-end flow.

## Who can access this app?

Treasury dealers and managers are the operational audience: they create and update deals and perform lifecycle actions such as cancellation, rollover handling, exit requests, maturing, and closing. Access is enforced per record, so a user only sees and acts on the deals their role and business relationships permit.

Confirmation carries a segregation-of-duties rule: the user who captured a deal cannot confirm it. A different eligible Treasury user must perform confirmation, and an attempt by the capturer is blocked and recorded as a security event. Client users may have permission to view deals linked to their business-partner relationships, and in some cases to create or update deals for their organization. These record-level permissions do not change the app's stated operational audience: Treasury dealers and managers.

## How does work progress in this app?

1. A user starts a new deal, which opens as a draft you can save and return to. Defaults are pre-filled for you—the company from your session, own-book acting capacity, the company's base currency, and a system-generated deal number—so capture focuses on the trade itself: the company, counterparty, instrument, trade and maturity dates, dealing capacity, business rationale, the deal's legs, and the instrument-specific terms that apply to the chosen instrument. Saving checks that the deal is ready to capture: the company must be live, the counterparty approved for trading, and the instrument activated for that company; on-behalf deals additionally require a registered client, a company operating model that permits on-behalf trading, an approved product scope covering the instrument, and the required conduct disclosures. Activating the draft is the capture moment: the deal moves from **Draft** to **Captured**, the transition is recorded in the deal's lifecycle history, and a capture event is added to the deal's evidence. Captured deals stay editable so terms can still be corrected before confirmation, and a deal's status only ever changes through the named lifecycle actions rather than by editing the field. Drafts left inactive expire automatically after a configured interval (72 hours by default). See the [capture and confirm task guide](./capture-and-confirm-a-deal) for the step-by-step flow, including what to enter for each deal type.
2. A different eligible Treasury user opens the captured deal and selects **Confirm Deal**. Confirmation is the pivotal step: the platform validates the deal's readiness, posts accounting entries, projects cashflow items, locks the contractual terms, seals the deal's evidence, and opens a position tracking the exposure. Which downstream records appear for a given deal depends on the company and instrument configuration that governs confirmation.
3. After confirmation, the deal can progress further depending on its state and your intent: it can be cancelled with a reason (while not yet in a terminal state), rolled over via a propose-and-approve flow, flagged with an exit request, or matured when it reaches its contractual maturity.
4. Settlement itself is processed through the back-office settlement and cashflow processes. Once a deal is settled, it can be closed in this app.

Treat this as an orientation to the workflow. Follow the task guide and any applicable approval procedure before changing a deal's lifecycle status.

## What can change what I see or do?

- **Instrument:** The deal presents the detail sections relevant to its instrument—such as **Foreign Exchange**, **Money Market**, **Loan Details** with a repayment schedule, or **Derivatives**—rather than a single fixed layout for every deal type.
- **Acting capacity:** A deal made on behalf of a client (**On Behalf**) can include client attribution and pre-hedging compliance information that does not apply to the company's own-book deals.
- **Lifecycle status:** Actions are gated by state. Confirming applies to captured deals; rollover and exit actions apply to confirmed deals (with pending-rollover state further switching which rollover actions appear); maturing applies to confirmed deals; closing applies to settled deals; cancelling is hidden once a deal is settled, cancelled, defaulted, or terminated. A deal with a pending rollover is locked against cancellation.
- **Access and compliance:** Available actions still depend on role and record-level authorization. Compliance policy can also block progress outright—for example, a blacklisted or KYC-expired counterparty, or a legal hold on the deal.

## Where can I review the results?

The deal page groups related information into **Deal Terms**, **Accounting Events**, **Cashflows**, **Rate History**, and **Evidence**. After confirmation, review the projected cashflow items and journal entries the platform generated, the deal's lifecycle history, and its sealed evidence packs. As settlement proceeds, bank confirmations and their matching against projected cashflows appear in the Evidence area. Rollovers, amendments, exits, and lineage link related deals together on the same page.

Do not assume every deal has the same downstream records: what exists depends on the instrument, the company's configuration, and how far processing has progressed. Related records are also maintained in their own apps—journal entries, positions, and settlements—where authorized; see the [settlement and cashflow guide](/user-guides/back-office/settlement-and-cashflow) for that handoff.

## What should I check if I can’t continue?

If an action is unavailable, check the deal's lifecycle status and your access first. For confirmation, also check that you are not the user who captured the deal. If the deal is locked, check for a pending rollover. If the deal cannot be saved or confirmed and the app shows a validation or policy message, resolve that message; compliance blocks require the underlying counterparty or hold issue to be addressed, not a workaround. The task guide covers the capture-and-confirm workflow; use the error guidance shown by the app for field-level blockers.

## Which task guides are available?
