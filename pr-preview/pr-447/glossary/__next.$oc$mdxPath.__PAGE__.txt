# Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Accrual | Recording income or expense in the period it is earned or incurred, regardless of when cash actually moves. |
| Acting Capacity | The role in which a bank executes a deal: on its own book, on behalf of a client, or in a hybrid combination of both. |
| All-In Rate | The complete exchange rate a client or book actually receives, combining the spot rate, forward points, and any spread or margin applied. |
| AML (Anti-Money Laundering) | Laws and controls designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income. |
| Approval Pool | The centralised worklist where authorised users approve or reject actions waiting on a governance gate. |
| Audit Log | A secure record of who did what in the system and when, designed to be tamper-evident and available to auditors and examiners. |
| BA700 | The South African regulatory return on banks assets, liabilities, and income, filed monthly with the regulator. |
| Basel III | The international framework strengthening bank capital, liquidity, and leverage requirements after the 2008 financial crisis. |
| Basis Point | One hundredth of one percent, the standard unit for quoting small changes in interest rates, spreads, and fee percentages. |
| BLACKLISTED Status | The highest counterparty restriction status, permanently blocking all deal activity with that business partner. |
| Cashflow Item | A projected or actual dated money movement generated from a deal, such as interest, principal, or exchange legs. |
| Cashflow Match | The process of linking an actual bank statement movement to the expected cashflow item it settles. |
| Commodity catalogue | The platform list of commodities a company may trade when commodity trading is enabled. |
| Company Onboarding Gate | The controlled checklist a company must pass before it can transact live on the platform. |
| Compliance programme | The written set of rules and controls a company keeps to meet its legal duties. |
| Conduct Flag | A marker raised on a partner or dealer when behaviour breaches conduct standards, prompting review before normal activity continues. |
| Counterparty Risk | The danger that the other side in a transaction fails to meet its obligations, causing a loss of the amount owed. |
| Credit Valuation Adjustment | The fair-value adjustment for the risk that a derivative counterparty defaults before the contract matures. |
| Deal Confirmation | The controlled second-person approval that turns a captured draft deal into a live, binding position on the platform. |
| Domain group | The subject area a list belongs to, for example ONBOARDING |
| Draft Document | A work-in-progress record that is saved continuously as the user fills a form, visible only to its creator until activation. |
| Drawdown | The act of taking funds from an approved credit or deposit facility, converting a committed limit into an actual outstanding balance. |
| DSAR (Data Subject Access Request) | A request by an individual to access, correct, or delete the personal data an organisation holds about them. |
| DV01 | The change in a position value for a one-basis-point move in interest rates, a linear measure of rate sensitivity. |
| ECP (Earning Credit Potential) | A regulatory add-on to counterparty credit exposure that reflects the potential future cost of replacing an FX contract if the counterparty defaults. |
| Effect Chain | The ordered sequence of downstream consequences a single deal event produces across the platform engines. |
| Effective Interest Rate | The rate that exactly discounts a financial instrument expected cashflows to its carrying amount, spreading interest income or cost evenly over the economic life. |
| EMIR | The European Market Infrastructure Regulation requiring OTC derivative reporting, clearing, and risk-mitigation controls. |
| ENG_ACC (Accounting Engine) | The pipeline step that resolves financial nodes, applies GL mapping, and posts journal entries for a deal event. |
| ENG_CASH (Cash Engine) | The pipeline step that projects cashflow items and updates cash positions and nostro balances for a deal event. |
| ENG_EVD (Evidence Engine) | The pipeline step that assembles and seals the evidence pack for a deal event. |
| ENG_LMT (Limit Engine) | The pipeline step that checks deal exposure against dealer authority and counterparty credit lines. |
| ENG_RISK (Risk Engine) | The pipeline step that computes exposure, market-risk metrics, and limit reservation for a deal event. |
| ENG_SAN (Sanitisation Engine) | The first pipeline step that normalises and validates incoming deal data before any business processing runs. |
| Exchange Control | Government restrictions on the movement of currency across borders, still partially applicable in South Africa for residents. |
| Expected Credit Loss | The probability-weighted estimate of credit losses a lender expects over the life of a financial instrument, recognised before any default actually occurs. |
| Exposure | The amount of credit or market risk currently at stake with a counterparty or on a position. |
| FATF | The Financial Action Task Force, the global body setting anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing standards. |
| FICA | The Financial Intelligence Centre Act, South Africa law requiring accountable institutions to verify clients, keep records, and report suspicious activity. |
| Financial Node | A named standard point in the accounting model where economic events attach, such as deal confirmation, accrual, or settlement, each with a defined debit and credit account pair. |
| FinSurv | The Financial Surveillance department of the South African Reserve Bank that administers exchange-control regulations and approvals. |
| Forward Exchange Contract | An agreement to exchange two currencies at a fixed rate on a future date, locking in the rate today to remove uncertainty about the future exchange rate. |
| Forward Points | The difference between the forward exchange rate and the spot rate for a currency pair, expressed in pips and driven by the interest-rate differential between the two currencies. |
| FSCA | The Financial Sector Conduct Authority, the South African regulator overseeing market conduct and the treatment of customers by financial institutions. |
| FX Forward | A bilateral agreement to buy or sell a currency at a predetermined rate on a set future date, used to hedge or speculate on currency movements. |
| FX Spot | A foreign exchange trade exchanging two currencies for settlement typically two business days after the trade date at the current market rate. |
| Gate | A requirement a company must meet before it may trade |
| Gate series | One of the five phases a gate belongs to: setup, instrument, operational, readiness or conditional. |
| General Ledger | The master set of accounts through which all financial transactions of an entity are recorded and from which financial statements are produced. |
| General Ledger Mapping | The configuration that decides which general ledger account receives each type of accounting event from each instrument. |
| Hash Chain | A sequence of records where each entry stores a cryptographic hash of the previous one, so altering any past record breaks every link after it. |
| Hedge Accounting | An optional IFRS treatment that aligns the accounting for a hedging instrument with the item it protects, so gains and losses offset in the same period. |
| Herstatt Risk | The risk that one leg of an FX trade settles while the counterparty fails before the second leg settles, named after a 1974 German bank collapse. |
| HQLA | High-quality liquid assets: cash and securities that can be converted to cash quickly with minimal loss in a stress scenario. |
| IFRS 9 | The international standard governing how financial instruments are classified, measured, provisioned for expected losses, and hedged. |
| Immutable Ledger | An append-only accounting record that can never be edited or deleted once written, with corrections made only by new offsetting entries. |
| Instrument Eligibility | The configuration that decides which instruments a company, desk, or dealer is permitted to trade. |
| ISO 20022 | The global standard for electronic data interchange between financial institutions, defining the XML and structured-data messages payments use. |
| JIBAR | The Johannesburg Interbank Amortised Rate, the benchmark interest rate at which South African banks lend to each other, published daily for standard tenors. |
| Journal Entry | A dated accounting record of a financial event, showing at least one debit and one matching credit to general ledger accounts. |
| KYC (Know Your Customer) | The process of verifying a client identity and assessing its risk before doing business, refreshed periodically. |
| LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio) | The ratio of high-quality liquid assets to net cash outflows under a 30-day stress scenario, a Basel III minimum standard. |
| Legal Hold | A directive that freezes records related to actual or anticipated litigation or investigation, prohibiting their alteration or deletion. |
| Licence requirement | A licence a company must hold before it may trade a given instrument. |
| Lifecycle Status | The controlled state of a deal from draft through confirmed, settled, and matured, governing which actions are legal at each point. |
| Limit Breach | A transaction or market move that takes exposure above an approved limit, requiring either prevention or documented override. |
| Limit Utilisation | The percentage of an approved credit or dealing limit currently consumed by live exposure. |
| Liquidity Ladder | A time-bucketed view of expected cash inflows and outflows showing how liquid the book is over days, weeks, and months. |
| Loan-to-Value | The ratio of a loan amount to the value of the asset securing it, used to judge credit risk and set conditions. |
| Mandatory position | A role a company must have by law or rule, such as a company secretary. |
| Mark-to-Market | Valuing an open position at the price it could be settled at today, replacing its original booked value with current market value. |
| Market Risk | The risk of loss from movements in exchange rates, interest rates, or other market prices while a position is open. |
| Material dependency | An outside provider whose failure would seriously disrupt the company. |
| Maturity Date | The date on which a financial contract ends and principal plus any final interest must be repaid or settled. |
| Money Market | The market for short-term borrowing and lending, typically overnight to one year, where banks and corporates manage daily funding and liquidity. |
| Mutual Banks Act | The South African statute governing mutual banks - deposit-taking institutions owned by and lending to their members. |
| NCA | The National Credit Act, South African law governing consumer credit agreements, affordability assessments, and debt-restructuring conduct. |
| Nostro Account | A bank account a institution holds with a foreign bank, denominated in that foreign currency, used to settle cross-border payments. |
| Nostro Reconciliation | The daily comparison of the expected nostro position of a bank with the actual balance reported by the correspondent bank, resolving differences. |
| Notional Amount | The face value of a derivative or funding contract on which payments are calculated, which is usually never exchanged between the parties. |
| P&L Attribution | The allocation of profit and loss to the desk, book, instrument, or client activity that generated it. |
| Period Close | The controlled process of finalising all accounting for a period, after which no further entries may post to it. |
| Pipeline domain | One of the nine areas of deal processing after go-live that a company can switch on. |
| Pipeline Engine | The ordered set of processing engines that evaluate, account for, settle, and evidence every deal event on the platform. |
| POPIA | The Protection of Personal Information Act, South Africa data-protection law governing how personal information may be collected, processed, and retained. |
| Posting | The act of recording a journal entry into the general ledger so it affects account balances and financial reports. |
| Regulatory framework | A body of law or rules that binds the company, for example a banking or financial-crime framework. |
| Repo Rate | The rate at which the South African Reserve Bank lends to commercial banks against collateral, the primary policy anchor for all rand interest rates. |
| Revolving Credit | A pre-approved credit facility a borrower can draw, repay, and redraw repeatedly during its life without new approval each time. |
| Risk appetite | The amount and kind of risk a company is willing to accept to reach its aims. |
| Rollover | Extending the settlement date of an existing FX or money-market position by closing the near leg and opening a new forward leg at updated rates. |
| SAMOS | The South African Multiple Option Settlement system, the real-time gross settlement system operated by the Reserve Bank for high-value rand payments. |
| Sanctions Screening | Checking clients and counterparties against government and international sanctions lists before allowing transactions. |
| SARB | The South African Reserve Bank, the central bank responsible for monetary policy, currency issuance, and stability of the financial system. |
| Segregation of Duties | A control requiring that no single person performs two conflicting steps of a process, such as capturing and confirming the same deal. |
| Settlement Instruction | The authorised direction to move funds for a deal, carrying amount, currency, accounts, and value date for execution. |
| SOFR | The Secured Overnight Financing Rate, the US benchmark for short-term dollar funding, replacing LIBOR for most USD contracts. |
| STR (Suspicious Transaction Report) | A confidential report filed with the Financial Intelligence Centre when a transaction appears to involve proceeds of crime or unusual activity. |
| SWIFT | The Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication, the messaging network banks use to send payment and trade instructions securely. |
| Tenor | The length of time until a financial contract matures or a rate reprices, quoted in days, months, or years. |
| Term Loan | A loan for a fixed amount over a fixed period, usually with scheduled interest payments and repayment at or before maturity. |
| Time Bucket | A standardised date range such as overnight or 30-90 days into which cashflows and exposures are grouped for analysis. |
| Trader Limit Matrix | The grid of dealing authorities per dealer and instrument type defining the maximum notional or exposure each may transact. |
| Treasury Deposit | Funds placed by one party with another for an agreed term and rate, generating interest income and a return of principal at maturity. |
| TUJ Entry | A trial-balance journal record produced by the accounting engine that summarises position movements for ledger proofing and downstream integration. |
| Value at Risk | The maximum expected loss on a portfolio over a given horizon at a stated confidence level, summarising market risk in one number. |
| Value Date | The date on which the cash flows of a transaction actually exchange or become interest-bearing, distinct from the trade date when the deal was agreed. |
| Volatility | The degree to which a rate or price fluctuates over time, usually expressed as an annualised standard deviation in percent. |
| Yield Curve | A line plotting interest rates against maturities for a given currency, showing the term structure of funding costs. |
