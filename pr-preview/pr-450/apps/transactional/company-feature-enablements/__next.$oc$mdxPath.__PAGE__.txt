# Company Feature Enablements app

Company Feature Enablements is where each company's gate behaviour is switched on, off or softened. One record per company controls whether readiness gates block work or only warn, and which individual checks — readiness, policy, KYC, business window, market data — are temporarily bypassed while the platform is being set up or tested.

## What is this app for?

Each record belongs to one company and carries two kinds of setting. **Gate Enforcement** decides how gates behave for that company: **Blocking** means gates are enforced and actions are held until the gates pass, while **Informational** means gates produce warnings without stopping anything. The **Bypass Flags** section holds the five bypass switches, each with its own expiry date, covering readiness gates, policy gates, the KYC gate, the business window and market data.

Bypasses are a temporary, auditable exception rather than a permanent configuration: every switch carries an expiry date no more than 30 days away, and switches that have run out can be cleared here in one action.

## When should I use this app?

Use it when a company needs its gates enforced differently — for example to let a non-production company move through setup without being stopped by every readiness check, or to relax enforcement for a short, explained period. It is also where you turn bypasses off again once the reason for them has passed. Day-to-day gate outcomes show up in the apps that apply the gates, and the gate definitions those apps use are held in the **Gate Catalogue**.

## Who can access this app?

The app is reached through portal access plus the roles your administrator has assigned you — see [Roles and Access](/getting-started/roles-and-access). At the service level, updating these records and running both bypass actions is granted to the **Treasury** role. Enablement records cannot be deleted.

## How does work progress in this app?

1. Open or create the enablement record for the company. The company and its **Environment** are fixed on the record, and the environment must match the company's own environment — the record takes the company's environment when it is created.
2. Choose **Gate Enforcement**. The record starts at **Blocking**. Moving it to **Informational** requires a reason, and the platform stamps the change with when it happened and who made it.
3. Switch on individual bypass flags only where you have a reason to. Each flag needs an expiry date no more than 30 days away; turning a flag off clears its expiry date.
4. When the temporary period is over, select **Deactivate All Bypasses** to switch off every bypass on the record at once, or **Deactivate Expired Bypasses** to clear bypasses whose expiry date has passed. The second action cleans up expired bypasses on every enablement record, not only the one you have open.
5. Save. The record itself has no approval step — the reason, timestamp and user in the **Audit Trail** are the accountability for a change of enforcement.

## What can change what I see or do?

- **Environment:** a company in **Production** refuses any bypass switch with a message, and production ignores bypass flags altogether. **UAT** and **Development** honour them.
- **Expiry dates:** a bypass cannot be switched on without an expiry, and an expiry further than 30 days out is refused. A bypass whose date has passed no longer applies, and **Deactivate Expired Bypasses** clears it.
- **Gate enforcement:** **Blocking** holds actions until gates pass; **Informational** warns instead of blocking. Only the switch to **Informational** demands a written reason.
- **Company and environment:** both are fixed after the record is created, so a record cannot be repointed at a different company.
- **Access:** writing and both actions remain limited to the **Treasury** role.

## Where can I review the results?

Check the record itself: the **Bypass Flags** section shows which switches are on and when each expires, and the **Audit Trail** section shows when **Gate Enforcement** last changed, who changed it and why. To see the effect, look at where a gate applies for that company — under **Blocking** the action is held until the gate passes, under **Informational** you get a warning instead.

## What should I check if I can't continue?

- A bypass will not switch on in production: that is by design. Production companies run with gates as configured and without bypasses.
- You are told an expiry date is required or too far away: set a date within the next 30 days.
- You are asked for a reason: it is mandatory when moving **Gate Enforcement** to **Informational**.
- A delete command is refused: enablement records are retained.
- The registered messages for this app are listed in the [block 12xx error reference](/reference/error-codes/block-12xx-cfe).

## Which task guides are available?

No task guide is published for this app yet; creating the record, setting enforcement, activating and clearing bypasses are covered on this page.
