# Glossary app

Glossary is where the platform's shared vocabulary is maintained. Each record is one term, built around its **Term** and optional **Abbreviation**, classified by **Category**, **Scope** and **Audience Level**, and defined in a **Short Definition** plus an **Extended Definition**.

## What is this app for?

The glossary exists so that a term means the same thing everywhere it appears. Alongside the two definitions, a term can carry a **Formula or Example**, a **Regulatory Reference**, **Related Terms**, a **Where Used** note naming the apps and pages where the term appears, and **Tags**.

**Category** says which subject area the term belongs to — FX and treasury trading, accounting and IFRS, risk management, South African or international regulation, cash and liquidity, compliance, platform architecture, loans and credit, or market data. **Scope** says which framework the definition comes from — global, South Africa, Kompas platform, IFRS or Basel. **Audience Level** says who it is written for — executive, treasury, accounting, technology, regulatory, or universal.

## When should I use this app?

Use it when a term is missing, wrong or out of date, when a new abbreviation needs recording, when a definition should be retargeted at a different audience, or when a term has been replaced and the successor should be named.

If you are unsure what a word means while you work, the published [Glossary](/glossary) is the quicker place to look it up — this app is where those entries are maintained.

## Who can access this app?

The app is reached through portal access plus the roles your administrator has assigned you — see [Roles and Access](/getting-started/roles-and-access). The app's own metadata records no additional role restriction and no narrower launchpad audience, so everyone who can open it sees the same terms.

## How does work progress in this app?

1. Find the term — the list shows **Term**, **Abbreviation**, **Category**, **Scope**, **Audience Level** and the two definitions side by side.
2. Select **Create** to add a term in draft, or open an existing one to correct it.
3. Classify it: choose **Category**, **Scope** and **Audience Level** from their lists.
4. Write the **Short Definition** first — the one-line explanation shown in lists and in the published glossary — then the **Extended Definition**, and add a **Formula or Example** or a **Regulatory Reference** where the term needs one.
5. Point the term at its context with **Where Used** and **Related Terms**. When a term is being replaced, name the replacement in **Superseded By** and set **Effective From**.
6. Save. To retire a term without losing it, switch **Active** off.

## What can change what I see or do?

- **Fixed value lists:** **Category**, **Scope** and **Audience Level** come from the platform's lists, so only the values your configuration offers are selectable. If one is missing, it is not something this page can add.
- **Active:** a retired term stays in the register and can still be read and corrected; only the flag changes.
- **Where Used and Related Terms are notes, not links:** they are free text that tells a reader where to look and which terms sit alongside this one. They do not create navigation, so they need to be kept current by hand.
- **Local language fields:** **Term Local Language**, **Definition Local** and the local language code and name are available for a translated version of the term, and are optional — a term does not need one.
- **Superseded By** names the replacement as text, so the replacement term must already be written before the reference makes sense.

## Where can I review the results?

The object page's **General Information** section holds every field of a term in one place, which is the best place to read a definition end to end with its example and regulatory reference.

The documentation site publishes a [Glossary](/glossary) built from this same term set, showing each term with its short definition — a useful check that your wording still reads well as a single line. **Where Used** also names the apps and pages where the term appears, so you can judge where a wording change will be noticed.

## What should I check if I can’t continue?

- A category, scope or audience level you expect is not offered: those choices come from the platform's fixed lists, not from this app.
- An edit control is missing: check your roles in [Roles and Access](/getting-started/roles-and-access).
