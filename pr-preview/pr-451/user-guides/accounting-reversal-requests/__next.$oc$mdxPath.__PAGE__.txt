# Accounting Reversal Requests app

Accounting Reversal Requests is where you ask for a posted accounting entry to be reversed, and where a second person decides on that ask. Each request names the original journal entry, states the reason for the correction, and records who requested it, who approved it and when — so the correction is traceable from the request to the reversal journal the platform creates.

## What is this app for?

The app manages one business object: a reversal request against an existing journal entry. A request must say what kind of correction it is — for example a straight **Reverse Exact**, a **Reverse and Replace**, or a **Forward Adjustment** — and must explain why the entry is being reversed. Once approved, the platform posts a reversal journal entry with the original postings in the opposite direction on the same accounts and currencies, and keeps the request as the audit record of who asked for and who authorised the correction.

## When should I use this app?

Use this app when a journal entry that has already been posted needs correcting and you want a governed, reviewable correction rather than an edit to history. Journal entries themselves are not created or edited here: you reference one as the **Original Journal** and review the outcome in the journal records afterwards. Reopening an accounting period is a separate action outside this app; a request of type **Prior Period Correction** or **Regulator Restatement** records your intent but does not reopen the period for you.

## Who can access this app?

Reversal request records are granted to the Treasury role at the service level, and the app itself is reached through portal access plus the roles your administrator has assigned you — see [Roles and Access](/getting-started/roles-and-access). The consequential step carries a two-person rule: the person who raised a request cannot be the person who approves or rejects it. An attempt by the requester is blocked with a message explaining that a different user must approve, and blocked attempts are written to the security audit log.

## How does work progress in this app?

1. You create a request in draft. Choose the **Reversal Type**, the **Original Journal** and a **Reason**; the reason and type are mandatory. When you save, the **Requester** is filled from your account, the **Request Date** is set, and the status starts at **Pending**. The type, requester, request date and original journal stay fixed after saving, so the correction cannot be quietly retargeted later.
2. A different user opens the saved request and selects **Approve** or **Reject**. Both actions are only offered on a saved request that is still **Pending**.
3. **Approve** records who approved and when, then creates the reversal journal entry linked to the original and moves the request to **Completed**, passing through **Approved** while the entry is created.
4. **Reject** records the rejection and stops there: the status becomes **Rejected** and no reversal journal entry is created.

Treat this as orientation. Follow your accounting procedure before approving a reversal.

## What can change what I see or do?

- **Status:** **Approve** and **Reject** appear only while the request is **Pending** and saved. A rejected or completed request is a closed decision and offers neither action.
- **Two-person rule:** if you are the requester, approval and rejection are refused even though the buttons are visible.
- **Record completeness:** a request cannot be saved without a **Reversal Type** and a **Reason**, and the reversal itself needs an original journal entry that has postings; a missing or incomplete original stops the reversal with an explanatory message.
- **Access:** available actions still depend on your role and record-level permissions.

## Where can I review the results?

The request itself shows **Status** in the list and an **Approval** section with who approved it and when. The meaningful outcome is the new reversal journal entry: open the journal records and follow the entry that is recorded as a reversal of the original, then compare the postings with the original entry. A rejected request shows the rejection on the request and leaves the original journal untouched.

## What should I check if I can't continue?

- The action is not offered: check whether the request is still saved and still **Pending**, then check your access.
- You are told a different user must approve: you are the requester; ask a colleague to decide.
- The request will not save: complete the **Reversal Type** and **Reason** and try again.

## Which task guides are available?

- [Request a reversal of a journal entry](./request-a-reversal-of-a-journal-entry)
- [Decide on an accounting reversal request](./decide-on-an-accounting-reversal-request)
